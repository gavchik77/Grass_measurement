Step 1 · Measure grass before allocating it

Step 1a · Use the 0.5 m × 0.5 m quadrat method

Before you can calculate Average Farm Cover or allocate grass to animals, you need an estimate of kg DM/ha in the paddock. In this exercise, start with the quadrat method.

Quadrat length0.5 m
Quadrat width0.5 m
Fresh grass cut0.200 kg
Grass DM16%

A. Why is the multiplier 40,000?

Quadrat area = 0.5 m × 0.5 m = 0.25 m²
Number of 0.25 m² quadrats in 1 ha = 10,000 m² ÷ 0.25 m²

B. Calculate paddock grass cover

Grass cover (kg DM/ha) = fresh grass weight (kg) × DM fraction × 40,000
Important: convert the DM percentage to a fraction before multiplying. For example, 16% = 0.16 — not 16.
Complete the quadrat calculation first. This gives the kg DM/ha value that can then be used in the grazing calculations.

Step 1b · Plate meter calculation

Take plate-meter heights across the paddock in a W or X pattern. For this exercise, subtract the desired post-grazing residual height, then multiply the difference by 250 kg DM/cm.

Grass cover (kg DM/ha) = (average paddock height − residual height) × 250
Average paddock height8.8 cm
Desired residual4.0 cm
DifferenceCalculate it
Multiplier250 kg DM/cm
Example: (8.8 − 4.0) × 250 = 1,200 kg DM/ha
Calculate the height above the residual first, then convert it to kg DM/ha.

Step 2 · Animal need

How much grass does the herd need?

For a very simple estimate, use approximately 3% of body weight as daily DM intake.

Daily grass DM per animal = body weight × 0.03
At 550 kg: about 16.5 kg DM/day. At 600 kg: 18 kg. At 650 kg: 19.5 kg. This is a simple teaching estimate.
Calculate the requirement from the values above.

Step 3 · Average Farm Cover

Estimate the average grass cover across the 10 paddocks

PaddockCover (kg DM/ha)Status vs target
AFC = total paddock cover ÷ number of paddocks
Why is this simple average valid here? All 10 paddocks are the same size: 12 ha ÷ 10 = 1.2 ha each. If paddocks had different areas, an area-weighted AFC would be more appropriate.
Add the 10 covers and divide by 10.

Step 5 · Daily demand and ideal pre-grazing yield

Use stocking rate, intake and rotation

Demand = stocking rate × intake
Ideal pre-grazing yield = (Demand × rotation) + residual

Step 7 · Grazing days ahead

Can the 40-cow herd graze each paddock for 2–3 days?

Extension herd: 40 cows × 16 kg DM/day = 640 kg DM/day.

Grazing days ahead = total DM ÷ 640
Grazing time per paddock = grazing days ahead ÷ 10
Compare your answer with the 2–3 day target.

Step 8 · Simple grass growth

How fast did an ungrazed paddock grow?

For a simple teaching estimate, compare two cover measurements from the same ungrazed paddock.

Growth rate = (current cover − previous cover) ÷ number of days
This simple formula is appropriate only when the paddock has not been grazed or cut between the two measurements.
Calculate the increase in cover and divide by the number of days.
